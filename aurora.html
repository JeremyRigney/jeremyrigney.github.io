WSA-Enlil is NOAA’s solar wind model. It starts from the Sun’s magnetic field and extends past Mars. Colour is radial speed in the plane of Earth’s orbit.

Streaks move at that speed; their number shows density. Earth stays fixed as the spiral turns past.

The model runs 0.1–1.7 AU, between the dashed rings. Inside and beyond them the wind is extrapolated.

“Ahead” is the next 3 days. The Sun and planets are not to scale.

The Sun from GOES-19

 

SUVI on GOES-19 images the Sun in extreme UV every 4 minutes. At 171 Å it shows coronal loops at ~600,000 K. Dark areas are coronal holes, the source of fast wind that reaches Earth 2–4 days later.

Corona adds CCOR-1, the coronagraph on the same spacecraft, every 15 minutes, in white (NOAA shows it in blue). Its occulter hides the inner 4.5 solar radii; its arm casts the dark band. CMEs show as bright fronts moving out.

The slider plays NOAA’s last 24 hours. The far side, unseen from GOES, is a plain glow.

      NOAA SWPC, the L1 monitors and MagIE. Coastlines from Natural Earth, heights from NOAA’s ETOPO1, night lights from NASA’s Black Marble 2016. Cloud from Open-Meteo (CC BY 4.0).

      Aurora from NOAA SWPC’s OVATION model, 30–90 minutes ahead. Solar wind from the L1 monitors; Kp, scales and alerts from SWPC. Magnetometers from MagIE (DIAS, Met Éireann and Geological Survey Ireland).

      Solar wind from the Sun: NOAA SWPC’s WSA-Enlil, downsampled for this page each time a new run is published. Near Earth, the magnetopause follows Shue et al. (1998) and the bow shock distance Farris & Russell (1994), both from the wind measured at L1. Field lines and flow are illustrative. Planet positions from JPL’s approximate orbital elements.

      Away from live, the time slider shows OVATION-Prime 2010 driven by NOAA’s observed and forecast Kp, the same approach as MagIE’s forecast animation. Each Kp maps to the median solar-wind coupling at that Kp in NASA’s OMNI data, 2012–2025, and the model is matched to NOAA’s hemispheric power near the present. It underestimates storms above ~Kp 6, and Kp forecasts more than a day out are uncertain. Magnetic coordinates: AACGM-v2.

      Kp (0–9) measures how disturbed Earth’s magnetic field is. From Ireland, aurora usually shows low in the north from Kp 5.

      Bz is the north–south component of the solar wind’s magnetic field. When it points south (negative), the wind’s energy gets in and the aurora grows.

      Power: NOAA’s estimate of the power going into the northern aurora.
